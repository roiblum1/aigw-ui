# 0.37.0 (not released yet)

A tenant can be linked to an LDAP group. Its members sign in on the
tenants' page with the hub cluster's own OpenShift login, make a personal
key of their own, and see what every member used. The gateways now count
each key, not only each tenant, so usage is shown per person, on the
tenants' page and the Tenants page. The link is optional: a tenant without
a group works by its keys alone, as before, and nothing is created from
LDAP.

This release also carries 0.36.1, which was not released on its own: the
Windows installer and self-updating clients (see [0.36.1](v0.36.1.md)).

Database: migration 037 (`tenants.sso_group`, `api_keys.owner`,
`usage_counter_cursors.key_id`, table `key_token_hours`).

## Upgrading

- Nothing changes until you turn it on. With `redis.tokenCounts` on (the
  default), the budget policy `aigw-ui-tenant-budgets` gets four more rules,
  which count each key. Its existing rules keep their positions, so no
  counter is renamed. The policy then has 9 rules instead of 5.
- **To let people sign in with OpenShift:**
  1. Check that the hub cluster's Groups are synced from LDAP (`oc get
     groups`).
  2. Upgrade with `sso.enabled=true`, which needs `route.enabled`. The chart
     makes the hub's ServiceAccount an OAuth client, with a redirect
     reference to the Route, and creates a ClusterRole to read the Groups.
     Creating that role needs cluster-admin; with
     `sso.createClusterRole=false`, grant it yourself.
  3. Link tenants to groups on the Tenants page.
- **A hub that CI upgrades** (`hack/deploy-hub.sh`, an account limited to
  the namespace) needs `sso.createClusterRole=false` and the role granted
  by hand (`docs/operations.md`): Helm reads the ClusterRole on every
  upgrade, and that account may not.
- The token endpoint defaults to the OAuth server's Service, which pods
  trust. If that cannot be reached, set `sso.tokenURL` to empty, which uses
  the Route, and give the Route's CA with `sso.caConfigMap` if the pod does
  not trust it.

## Added

- **A tenant's LDAP group**: `sso_group` on `POST` and `PUT /tenants`,
  unique and optional. You can set it on the Tenants page when adding a
  tenant, or later on the tenant's page, which offers the Groups the hub
  read and warns when the cluster has none by that name.
- **Sign in with OpenShift** on `/my-usage`:
  - `GET /sso/login` and `GET /sso/callback` run OAuth with the hub's
    ServiceAccount as the client, asking for `user:info` with PKCE.
  - The session is a signed cookie that lasts 12 hours.
  - The person sees the enabled tenants of their groups, with a picker when
    there are several.
  - `/my/usage` and `/my/models` take the session, with the tenant in
    `X-Aigw-Team`.
  - New routes: `GET /api/v1/sso`, `POST /api/v1/sso/logout`, and `GET
    /api/v1/sso/groups` for the admin.
- **Your own key.** `GET` and `POST /my/key`: a person makes one personal
  key per tenant, which a new one replaces. The page shows it once with the
  two setup lines.
- **Leaving the group.** Every minute the hub reads the Groups. It revokes
  the key of anyone no longer in their tenant's group, and syncs
  (`key.revoke`). When the Groups cannot be read, or the group is missing,
  it revokes nothing.
- **Usage by person**:
  - The gateways count each key's requests and input, cached and output
    tokens by model and hour, by the client ID its tokens carry
    (`render.KeyCounterKey`, rules 5 to 8).
  - The hub keeps them in `key_token_hours`.
  - `GET /my/people` shows every member of the group each key's use and
    cost over a day, a week or 30 days. `GET /tenants/{id}/key-usage`
    shows the same to the admin.
  - The Tenants page shows who made each key.
- **Running the hub outside a pod:** `SSO_API_SERVER`, `SSO_TOKEN_FILE` and
  `SSO_NAMESPACE`, with a token of the ServiceAccount.

## Tested

On 2026-10-26, on macOS:

- `go test -race ./...` with Postgres 17 and Redis 7. This covers:
  - the store: groups, one live key per person, `key_token_hours`, cursors
    that carry the key, the keys counted;
  - per-key counters read and recorded beside the tenant's;
  - the sign-in against a stand-in OAuth server and API over TLS:
    - PKCE, the code exchange with the ServiceAccount's token, `users/~`;
    - Groups kept across a failed read;
    - signed cookies: changed, expired, wrong purpose or key;
  - revocation of someone who left, once, and nothing revoked while the
    Groups cannot be read or the group is missing;
  - the routes: sign-in, a wrong state, 503 before the Groups are read,
    403 for another group's tenant or a tenant without a group, the key
    made and replaced, every key's use, 403 for a key sign-in, sign-out;
  - the admin's group on add and change: 409 for a taken group, and a
    refused change leaves the tenant as it was.
- `hack/test-apiserver.sh`, and its variant with Envoy Gateway v1.9.1 and
  AI Gateway v1.1.0: the budget policy with its 9 rules was taken, with the
  per-key rules' two Distinct headers kept.
- The web tests (usage-by-person rows and costs), `tsc` and the build.
  `hack/check-chart.sh` renders the chart with the sign-in on, and refuses
  it without a Route.
- In a browser (Brave, headless), a local hub against a stand-in for the
  OAuth server and the API:
  - alice signed in with OpenShift, saw her two teams and made her key;
  - usage by person showed her and the admin's key, and the admin's page
    showed the group, the usage by key with costs ($0.72 and $0.22, checked
    by hand) and the group field when adding a tenant;
  - the page had no sideways scroll at phone width;
  - taken out of the group in the stand-in, her key was revoked at the next
    look, and the task log shows `key.revoke`, succeeded.

Live, on 2026-10-26 (from 23:41 UTC on the 25th), on the sandbox hub
(OpenShift 4.20.35, htpasswd logins, no LDAP) and the lab's gateway, after
CI deployed `6720c4d`:

- CI's deploy, with its namespace account, upgraded the hub to 0.37.0;
  migration 037 ran at start, no restart. A server-side dry run as that
  account with sign-in on failed ("cannot get resource
  clusterrolebindings"), and passed with `sso.createClusterRole=false`.
  The hub was turned on that way, with the role made by hand.
- **Sign-in with the real OAuth server.** It took the ServiceAccount as
  the client, with its redirect reference (`client_id`
  `system:serviceaccount:aigw-ui:aigw-ui`, `scope` `user:info`, S256).
  The cluster's login page and a one-time approval of the scope led back
  to `/my-usage`, naming the user and the team. The code was traded at the
  OAuth server's Service, the chart's default. The session cookie is
  HttpOnly, Secure, Lax, 12 hours.
- **The Groups.** The first read came before the role existed: it got 403
  and revoked nothing. The next, a minute later, listed both Groups. A
  second tenant on the same group got 409. With the session, a tenant
  without a group got 403.
- **Own key.** Made on the page, then set up with the page's own `curl`
  line in an empty home, using the installer the hub served. It was
  answered through the gateway. After **Make a new key**, the first key's
  renewal got 401 ("ask for a new one"), and its last token was refused at
  the gateway (403) after the sync.
- **Counting per key on a live gateway.** The key's counters are under
  rules 5 to 8
  (`…_rule-5-match-0_<client id>_rule-5-match-1_qwen-tiny_<hour>`). They
  equalled the tenant's rules 1 to 4:
  - first 4 requests (1 answered, 3 refused by the model's limit of one a
    day), 32 input, 0 cached and 10 output tokens;
  - later 7 requests, 128 input and 44 output tokens.

  **Usage by person** and the admin's **Usage by key** showed the same,
  with $0.00002 (checked by hand), at 1280 and 390 px wide with no
  sideways scroll.
- **Leaving the group.** The user was taken out of the Group at 00:07:34
  UTC. The key was revoked 58 s later ("left e2e-r037-group", one task,
  succeeded), and its token was refused at the gateway at 63 s. The page
  then said the person's groups link no team.
- The cluster self-test passed, with the 9-rule budget policy, including
  "The tenant's requests and tokens are counted by model". Its warnings
  were about the model's pool and its cache.

Found by the live run and fixed before the release:

- Each key a person made was written twice to the task log: a finished
  task beside the sync's.
- The 403 for a tenant not among a person's said they were in no tenant's
  group at all.
- Documented: the requests counters, the key's and the tenant's (since
  0.30.0), also count requests that a limit refused.
