# 0.13.0 (not released yet)

A **Capacity** page: measure what one instance of a model serves at the
latency you promise, and see what the model can be given a day, how much of
that the tenants' budgets take, and the prices that pay for its instances.
See [capacity](../capacity.md).

## Measuring an instance

- **Measure** on the Capacity page starts AIPerf in a Job on the cluster,
  aimed at one ready pod of the model, past the gateway and the scheduler.
- It replays made-up coding-agent sessions whose prompts average about
  100,000 tokens, with a shared system prompt, repository files and a
  growing history, at 1, 2, 4 … requests in flight, and stops at the first
  step that misses the targets for the time to first token or between
  tokens.
- The Job prints one line of numbers per step. The hub reads them from the
  pod's log, so a measurement goes on while the hub restarts. Nothing of a
  prompt reaches the hub, and no tenant is charged.
- The share of prompt tokens served from the cache comes from the pod's own
  counters.

## What the model can be given

For the latest measurement of each model the page shows:

- what one instance serves: requests in flight, tokens a second, cache share;
- what the model can be given a day, in its unit: one instance's day ×
  ready instances × the expected busy share;
- what the tenants' budgets add up to a day, and the overcommit;
- the busiest day of the last 30 days against what every instance serves;
- with the cost of an instance an hour, the prices that pay for the
  instances, and what one busy instance is charged at the model's prices.

## Setting it up

- New chart value `capacity.image`: an AIPerf image the LLM clusters can
  pull. Empty turns measurements off. `deploy/aiperf/Containerfile` builds
  one with AIPerf 0.13.0 and its tokenizer inside, and the offline bundle
  now carries it; `load-images.sh` prints the value to set.
- The hub creates the Job with the kubeconfig it already holds for the
  cluster, in the namespace of the model's deployment.
- A database migration adds the measurements and the number of ready
  instances per site.

## Tested

- The arithmetic, the choice of the result step, the Job and the reading of
  its log, by unit tests.
- The Job's script, run against a stand-in for AIPerf and a stand-in model
  server: the steps, the cache share from the counters and the stop at the
  first missed target.
- The following of a Job to its end, for each way it can end, against a
  stand-in cluster.
- The new queries against Postgres.
- Not run on a GPU. Real figures for 100,000-token prompts need one.
