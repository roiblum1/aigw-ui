# 0.32.1 (2026-10-17)

Fixes 0.32.0's token policy, which stopped a cluster's proxy from taking any
change. 0.32.0 refused runs of denied tokens with a CEL rule that used a
comprehension (`exists()`) and joined strings; Envoy builds CEL without
either and refused the whole route configuration, so after the upgrade no
revoke, new quota or other change reached the lab's proxy, while Envoy
Gateway showed the policy `Accepted`. The rule is now a chain of
comparisons that Envoy 1.39.1 builds and enforces, tested on a real Envoy.

Database: no migration.

## Upgrading

- From 0.32.0: the hub's first sync replaces the `revoked-span` rules, and
  the proxy then takes every change it refused since 0.32.0's sync: revokes,
  quotas, models. Check the self-test passes, and that the proxy's
  `update_rejected` counters stop growing ([operations](../operations.md#troubleshooting)).
- From 0.31.0 or earlier: as 0.32.0's upgrade, without its fault.

## Fixed

- **The `revoked-span` rule is CEL Envoy builds.** Each span is
  `exp >= from && exp <= to` on the token's claims, joined with `||`; a
  stored token is told from a personal key's by `cid.endsWith(jti)` instead
  of `cid == sub + '.' + jti`. Envoy turns comprehensions off and does not
  join strings unless told to (Envoy 1.39 `filters/common/expr/evaluator.cc`),
  and Envoy Gateway does not tell it.
- **A span stands for ten or more denied tokens**, not three: an entry of two
  comparisons is about as long as eight `jti`s. 1,000 denials from 15
  deletions of test tenants make 5.3 KB and 99 lines of policy (0.32.0's
  form: 2.9 KB; as `jti`s: 33.6 KB and 1,113 lines). A rule holds about 19
  spans.

## Added

- **The tests check every authorization rule in CEL for what Envoy builds**
  (`TestAuthorizationCELForEnvoy`): no comprehension, and only the
  operators and string tests Envoy has. 0.32.0's rule fails it.

**Denied tokens in spans hold on the lab's Envoy (check 19b).** The proxy's
`update_rejected` counters stayed at 40 (10 on each of four route
configurations, from 0.32.0) through the whole run.

- Twelve test tenants deleted at once, their tokens issued with no other
  token in between: tokens 03 to 12 became one `revoked-span` from the
  `exp` of 03 to the `exp` of 12, exactly; 01 and 02 stayed `jti`s; the
  token of a live tenant issued between 02 and 03 ended the span. All
  twelve were refused with 403 within 16 s, the live one was answered, and
  `denied_spans` went from 1 to 2. The policy went from 144 to 143 lines.
- The span left by the first attempt's 1,016 deleted tenants (no `jti`
  listed for any of them) refused all 50 of their tokens, the one expiring
  on the span's last second included.
- A first set of twelve was issued while the seeding issued tokens: two
  live tokens expired inside the run, which split it into two runs of five,
  so they stayed `jti`s (all twelve refused). That is the rule: a span never
  covers a live token.

Held (checks 1 to 11, 13, 14, 16 to 20, 22 to 32): a quota counts cost + 1
and refuses with the pool spent; a budget counts both models' cost and

Failed or found:

- **History (check 21), one case short.** A tenant on amount 3, the only one
  on it, used 4 and was moved to 33 within 17 s: the rule for 3 went, 33
  took its place, and the history counted 1 of the 5. The history reads a
  former amount only under a rule that still exists, and a gone key only if
  a look saw it before; looks are a minute apart. Every listed case held:
  budget tenants' day rows (15 of 15, the tenant whose requests came just
  before seeding added five amounts included; the budget rule stayed at
  index 4), two amounts of one tenant (9 and 22), a reset after 3 requests.
- **A hand model on a fleet model's workload Service takes its
  deployment.** With `e2e-alias` pointed at `qwen-tiny`'s Service, the hub
  gave the LLMInferenceService to `e2e-alias`: `qwen-tiny` showed no site
  and no capacity, and a capacity run on it was refused (404). Both
  endpoints match the reported model, and the last one read writes the
  deployment row (`store.ApplyCapacity`, no order). In the first attempt it
  went the other way. The measurement (check 16) and the planner (check 17,
  to the credit) were done through `e2e-alias`.
- Check 23: regular tenants were served before every queued best-effort
  request in all three rounds, but the mean queue time was 15.7 s for
  best-effort against 16.7 and 17.3 s for regular, as two best-effort
  requests a round find free slots. One best-effort request waited out the
  60 s TTL (429). Fair within a class: no effect, as expected with the
  site's strict fairness; the hub's sync note says so.
- Cancelled requests: a non-streaming request cut by the client, and one cut
  before its first token, are charged nothing; vLLM stops in both. A client
  that leaves before the usage chunk pays the whole answer.
- Lowering an own quota does not bind the running hour (24c: 20 in the hour
  on an amount of 14 lowered to 6).
- A budget tenant's history row is its budget pass counter, cost + 1 a
  request, while the budget is charged the cost alone.
