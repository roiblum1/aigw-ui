# 0.26.0 (2026-10-16)

A model's standard budget is gone. A tenant has what it is given: one
budget across models, any amount, or quotas of its own per model, any
amounts. A tenant with neither draws from the model's pool. The standard
gave every tenant the same amount through one rule so that most tenants
needed no rule of their own; since 0.24.0 quotas take one rule per amount,
so a thousand tenants with a few amounts take a few rules anyway.

Database: migration 032 drops the standard's columns from `models`: every
standard, and every change of one waiting for its day, is gone.

## Upgrading

- **A tenant that relied on a standard draws from the pool.** A tenant with
  neither a quota of its own nor a budget across models on a model is held
  by the model's pool from the first sync after the upgrade. Give such
  tenants a quota or a budget across models first, or set the pool to what
  they should share.
- The first sync rewrites each model's `QuotaPolicy` without the standard's
  rule: the rules after it move up one position, so the counts of own
  quotas and the pool's count start again, as at 0.24.0.
- The rule that lets a tenant with a budget across models through holds
  4,294,967,295 a day instead of a minute, so its count per tenant is what
  the tenant used of the model; that count starts at the upgrade.

## Removed

- The **Standard budget** button and dialog on the Models page, and the
  model row's standard, waiting change and "No standard" mark. The row
  still shows how many rules the model's `QuotaPolicy` has.
- `PUT /models/{id}/standard` and `DELETE /models/{id}/standard/pending`
  (404 now); `standard`, `standard_next` and `standard_held` in
  `GET /models`. `rules_per_tenant` is how many rules the tenants' quotas
  would take with a rule each.
- On the tenant page, the note that a quota adds to the standard and the
  list of models a tenant used on the standard alone.

## Changed

- A tenant with a budget across models is counted on its own on each model
  with prices, under the rule that lets it through: the Usage page shows
  what it used of the model "in its budget across models", and the usage
  history keeps it per model, as the standard's count did.
- The self-test's tenant-header step checks the tenant's amount rule: a
  request whose client names another tenant in `x-aigw-tenant` is counted
  for the token's tenant and nothing for the other. It resets the
  tenant's spent quota first, as the client-ID step does. The quota step no
  longer skips a model with a standard.
- Creating an API key is refused only while a tenant has a budget across
  models or a list of models.
